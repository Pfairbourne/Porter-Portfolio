Every small company I've worked with has had one person who held it together. They weren't always the smartest in the room. They weren't always the loudest. They were the one who could pull the unit economics spreadsheet open, find the line item that was breaking margin, hop on a customer call, and come back with a fix for both.

I think this role is dramatically underrated.

## What it looks like

Concretely, the operator who can hold both:

- Can read a P&L without flinching.
- Has talked to 50+ customers in the last six months.
- Can write a clear paragraph.
- Knows which metric matters this quarter and why.
- Will write the doc rather than wait for someone to assign it.

None of these are "product" skills or "ops" skills or "finance" skills. They're the skills of someone who has decided that the company's problems are their problems.

## Why it matters

The bottleneck in most small companies isn't engineering velocity or marketing spend. It's the gap between "we know what's wrong" and "we've decided what to do about it." That gap is closed by people who can hold context across the spreadsheet *and* the call.

If you're building a team, hire one of these before you hire your third engineer.
