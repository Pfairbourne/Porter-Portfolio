PM leverage in the agent era

April 11, 2026 productagents

Most of the PM job is translation. You translate customer pain into a problem statement, problem statements into specs, specs into roadmaps, roadmaps into status updates, status updates into the next quarter’s plan. Translation is mechanical work that requires judgment — exactly the shape of work LLMs are getting good at.

The PMs who’ll have the most leverage in 2026 are the ones who treat their job as a series of small agents to be built, not a series of meetings to be attended.

Three places to start

Customer call digests. I haven’t written a customer call summary by hand in a year. The agent transcribes, extracts decisions, drafts a follow-up, and files it where the team will see it. Cost: ten minutes to build, hours back per week.

Status updates. Every Monday I push a button and get a draft of “what shipped, what slipped, what’s next” pulled from Linear, Slack, and the last week of design files. I edit; I don’t write.

PRD scaffolds. Not the thinking. The scaffold — the structure, the standard sections, the related-work links. The thinking is still mine.

What this isn’t

It’s not “delegate everything to AI.” The thinking is the job. The translation is the overhead. Build agents that eat the translation; spend your saved time on the thinking.
